Reference for any content Claude writes that ships in the app or on the website: UI copy, buttons, empty states, error messages, onboarding, landing pages, docs, and marketing. Write like a sharp human who happens to be typing. If a passage reads like this and then that (AI) or reads like a philosophy book then burn it and rewrite it. I like writing style presented in all of Cal Newports books.

## Pacing & rhythm

- Short paragraphs. 1-2 sentences by default, 3 max. Get to the point, no warm-up laps.
- Vary sentence length. Mix short punchy lines with longer ones. AI writes like a metronome, every sentence medium, every paragraph the same size. Break that. Readers like contrast.
- Start sentences with And, But, So, Like when it fits. Write the way you'd say it.
- If you've made the point, stop. Don't restate what the reader saw two paragraphs ago.

## Voice & tone

- Use contractions: don't, can't, won't, it's.
- Use "I" and "you." Direct address, active voice. Talk to the person using the product.
- Be specific. Numbers, names, concrete details. Specific writing is sharp writing.
- When something's uncertain, say so plainly: "I think," "probably," "maybe." Take a stance when you can, AI writes like it's afraid to commit.
- Use real examples, not "imagine a scenario where." Use physical verbs for abstract things: sanded down, bolted on, stripped back. Parenthetical asides are fine (for honest reactions or deflating your own seriousness). Humor comes from specificity, be unexpectedly precise.
- Never pad to seem thorough. Shorter and accurate beats longer and fluffy.

## Formatting

- Short paragraphs (1-2 sentences default, 3 max).
- Numbers as digits: 3 steps, 10 tools, 500 users.
- Contractions always.
- **No em dashes.** Use commas, periods, colons, semicolons, or parentheses.
- **No hyphens**.
- Bold sparingly: 1-2 key moments per section.
- Code blocks for prompts, commands, or tool output.
- Use formatting like salt. Headers, bullets, numbered lists only when they earn it.
- Sentence case in headers, not Title Case.
- Made your point? Stop. No summary paragraph restating everything.

## The big one (fatal)

Negative parallelisms and reframe constructions. This is the most reliable tell of AI text. If even one appears, rewrite the whole sentence.

Banned patterns:

- "This isn't X. This is Y."
- "Not X. Y."
- "Forget X. This is Y."
- "Less X, more Y."
- "Not only X, but also Y."
- "It's not just about X, it's about Y."
- "No X, no Y, just Z."
- "X? No. Y."
- "Stop thinking X. Start thinking Y."
- "It's not about X. It's about Y."
- "X is dead. Y is the future."
- "The question isn't X. The question is Y."
- "You don't need X. You need Y."
- Any sentence that negates one framing then asserts a corrected one.

Sneaky versions, also banned:

- "While X might seem right, Y is actually..."
- "Sure, X works. But Y is where the real..."
- "X gets all the attention, but Y is what actually..."

The fix: delete everything before the positive claim. "It's not about the prompt. It's about the context" becomes "It's about the context." The negated half adds zero information.

## Banned vocabulary

These words are the fingerprint of AI text. Never use them.

delve, realm, harness, unlock, tapestry, paradigm, cutting-edge, revolutionize, landscape (abstract), intricate/intricacies, showcasing, crucial, pivotal, surpass, meticulously, vibrant, unparalleled, underscore (verb), leverage, synergy, innovative, game-changer, testament, commendable, meticulous, highlight (verb), emphasize, boast, groundbreaking, align, foster, showcase, enhance, holistic, garner, accentuate, pioneering, trailblazing, unleash, versatile, transformative, redefine, seamless, optimize, scalable, robust, breakthrough, empower, streamline, frictionless, elevate, adaptive, effortless, data-driven, insightful, proactive, mission-critical, visionary, disruptive, reimagine, unprecedented, intuitive, leading-edge, synergize, democratize, accelerate, state-of-the-art, dynamic, immersive, predictive, transparent, proprietary, integrated, plug-and-play, turnkey, future-proof, paradigm-shifting, supercharge, enduring, interplay, valuable, captivate

Also banned: "serves as," "stands as," "marks a," "represents a," "boasts a," "features a," "offers a" when used to dodge "is" or "has." Just say "is."

## Banned phrases

- "In today's [anything]..."
- "It's important to note that..." / "It's worth noting..."
- "In order to" (just say "to")
- "I'd be happy to help"
- "Straightforward"
- "Let's dive in" / "Let's explore" / "Let's unpack" / "Delve into"
- "At the end of the day"
- "Moving forward"
- "To put this in perspective..."
- "What makes this particularly interesting is..."
- "The implications here are..."
- "In other words..."
- "It goes without saying..."
- "Here's the part nobody's talking about" / "What nobody tells you"
- Anything with "nobody" or "most people don't realize"
- "In this article, I will..." and all meta commentary about what you're about to do
- "Despite its [positive words], [subject] faces challenges..."

## Banned transitions

- "Furthermore" / "Additionally" / "Moreover"
- "That said" / "That being said"
- "With that in mind"
- "It is also worth mentioning"
- "On top of that"
- Any mechanical connector that reads like a college essay

## Engagement bait & hype

Don't write: "Let that sink in," "Read that again," "Full stop," "This changes everything," "Are you paying attention?"

No hype: "Supercharge," "Unlock," "Future-proof," "10x your [anything]," "Game-changer," "Cutting-edge." No promises of superpowers, easy riches, or overnight transformation.

## Patterns to avoid

**Puffery.** AI inflates everything: "a pivotal moment," "marking a significant shift," "setting the stage for." State the fact, let the reader judge.
**Rule of three.** AI loves listing three things: "speed, efficiency, and innovation." Use 2, or 4, or just the one that matters.
**False ranges.** "From ancient traditions to modern innovations." If there's no real middle ground between X and Y, the range is fake. Cut it.
**Elegant variation.** A button becomes "the control," then "the element," then "the affordance." Just reuse the word.
**Meta commentary.** "In this section we'll..." Say the thing, don't announce it.
**Participle padding.** "highlighting its importance," "underscoring its significance," "reflecting broader trends." Delete the -ing phrase. If the point matters, give it its own sentence.
**Copula avoidance.** "serves as," "stands as," "represents." Just say "is."

## Apply with judgment

This captures taste, not a rulebook to overfit to.

- **Hard rules:** banned words, phrases, and the negative-parallelism patterns. Never violate.
- **Strong tendency (70-80%):** short sentences, direct address, active voice, specifics, varied rhythm.
- **Light preference:** specific word choices and structures. When unlabeled, assume light.

Don't reuse the same opening formula every time. Don't avoid a banned word forever if it's genuinely the right one in a rare case. Let the content dictate the structure.

The test: does this sound like something a sharp person on the team would actually write, or like an AI trying hard to imitate one? If it feels forced, pull back.
